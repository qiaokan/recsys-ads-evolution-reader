The gist. Recommendation and advertising are one problem seen from two sides: choose, for one person at one moment, a few items out of millions, using feedback that the previous choices produced. Every generation of algorithms was forced by a bottleneck in the one before it: too little signal, too little model capacity, the wrong objective, too slow a serving path, a pricing rule that rewarded the wrong behaviour, or a measurement that could not tell correlation from cause. Read each era as an answer to the previous era's bottleneck, and each claim with its evidence grade attached.

One problem, two business models

A recommender and an ad system solve the same allocation problem; they differ in who supplies the items and who pays for the slot. A recommender ranks items the platform owns or hosts (videos, products, posts) to maximize some long-run user value. An ad system ranks items that third parties pay to insert, so the ranking score multiplies a predicted response by a price the advertiser sets or the platform derives, and the platform must also run an auction, charge a price, pace a budget (spread the advertiser's spend over the day) and prove that the ads caused sales. Since about 2016 the two have converged: the same deep ranking models, the same retrieval stack and, in 2024–2025, the same generative architectures serve both organic and paid slots at Meta, Google, ByteDance, Kuaishou and Alibaba.

Throughout, an impression is one display of one item to one user; a conversion is the action the advertiser ultimately wants (an install, a purchase, a sign-up); CTR (click-through rate) is clicks per impression; CVR (conversion rate) is conversions per click unless stated otherwise. Ads are priced per 1,000 impressions (CPM, cost per mille), per click (CPC) or per conversion (CPA, cost per action or acquisition); eCPM is expected revenue per 1,000 impressions whatever the pricing, the common currency for ranking ads; ROAS (return on ad spend) is attributed revenue divided by ad spend. The companion Computational Advertising reader covers contracts, cash flow and auctions in depth; this reader follows how the algorithms changed over time.

Pitfall: hindsight ordering

The table reads like a clean sequence, but generations overlapped for years. Logistic regression ran production ad ranking at Google well after deep models were published, item–item tables still power “customers also bought” lists, and gradient-boosted trees still win many tabular tasks. A new method usually displaced the old one first in the stage where its bottleneck hurt most.

Not everything is a click classifier: what each model predicts

Binary click and conversion classifiers dominate ranking, especially ad ranking; the other families regress ratings, rank pairs, classify over the whole catalogue, generate tokens or optimize bids. The binary classifier dominates ad ranking for a specific reason: the auction multiplies the prediction by a bid (chapter 3), so it needs a calibrated probability, not just a good ordering. Same data, different label

One impression log supports all of these. Predicting whether an impression was clicked gives a binary label; predicting the watch time after the click gives a regression label; pairing it with an unclicked item on the same page gives a pairwise label; treating the clicked item as the answer among the whole catalogue gives a multi-class label; and the sequence of a user's clicks gives next-token labels. Choosing the label is choosing what the system optimizes.

Five eras, each answering the last one's bottleneck

The eras are defined by the bottleneck their methods removed, not by calendar years; the dates are when the defining work appeared.

  1. 1992–2005
    Neighbourhoods and keyword auctions. Bottleneck removed: no way to personalize at all. Collaborative filtering predicts from other people's ratings; GoTo.com and Google AdWords sell clicks on search keywords by auction.
  2. 2006–2013
    Factorization and planet-scale linear models. Bottleneck removed: sparsity. Latent factors generalize across users and items that never co-occurred; hashed logistic regression trained online handles billions of ad features.
  3. 2013–2020
    Deep learning and the multi-stage funnel. Bottleneck removed: hand-built feature crosses and a single scoring stage. Embeddings, learned interactions and two-tower retrieval scale to billions of items.
  4. 2009–2025
    Exchanges, first-price auctions and autobidding (ads track; Era IV; the chapter also covers precursors from 2007 and events to 2026) and bias, causality and privacy (cross-cutting, with roots back to 2002). Bottlenecks removed: advertisers bidding by hand, and models trained on their own biased logs; then a new constraint imposed from outside: loss of cross-app identifiers.
  5. 2021–2026
    Scaling and generative recommenders. Bottleneck removed: ranking models whose quality stopped improving with more compute. Sequence transducers and generative retrieval show compute-scaling curves similar to language models; chapter 9 covers how large language models themselves are used across recommendation and ads.

Every claim carries an evidence grade

Industrial recommender research mixes peer-reviewed results with marketing; this reader labels which is which. The four grades:

GradeWhat it meansHow much to trust it
PaperPeer-reviewed or widely cited paper with the method describedThe method is as described. Reported online gains are the authors' A/B tests on their own baselines and cannot be reproduced outside the company.
PostCompany engineering blog, product documentation, press release or contemporaneous news recordDates and architecture outlines are usually accurate; performance numbers are selected and baselines unspecified.
CallEarnings call, investor letter or press statementDirectionally informative about what management attributes revenue to; not evidence of mechanism.
InferenceThis reader's interpretationArgued from the sources but not stated in them; flagged explicitly.

A recurring lesson from chapter 5: several celebrated neural gains shrank or vanished once baselines were tuned properly. Treat an unreproduced online uplift as a hypothesis.
