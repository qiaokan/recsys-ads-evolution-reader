The timeline: every dated milestone, 1992–2026

In this chapter

  • Filter the milestones by track (recommendation, ads, systems, evaluation and bias, privacy) and by era, or search by keyword.
  • For each milestone read what changed, why it mattered and its evidence grade, then follow the link to the chapter that explains it.
  • Notice the lags: a method's paper, its production adoption and its displacement are often years apart.
  • Terms are defined at first use here and collected in the glossary.

The gist. Read the timeline once from top to bottom with all tracks on: the recommendation and ads tracks run in parallel until about 2016 and then merge into one modelling stack, while the privacy track arrives from outside and reshapes the ads track after 2017. Then filter to a single track to see its internal logic: each entry answers a limitation of an earlier one.

Explore

Era IV (exchanges, autobidding, bias and privacy) overlaps eras II–V by date, so its milestones are found through the track filters. Evidence grades: Paper Post Call, defined in chapter 1. Each entry spells out its own abbreviations, since a filter may show it alone; conference and journal names (KDD, RecSys, ICML and so on) are expanded in the glossary.

  1. 1992
    Tapestry coins “collaborative filtering” Paper

    Xerox PARC users annotate messages and filter by other people's annotations. Collaborative, but users must name whom to trust. Ch. 3

  2. 1994
    Clickable banner ads on HotWired Post

    October 1994; the AT&T banner is the conventionally cited first. Ads are sold per thousand impressions (CPM, cost per mille) with no prediction of how users will respond. Ch. 3

  3. 1994
    GroupLens automates the neighbourhood Paper

    Resnick et al., CSCW: correlation-weighted, mean-centred prediction of Usenet ratings; the user–user template. Ch. 3

  4. 1995
    Ringo: social filtering for music Paper

    Shardanand and Maes, CHI: compares similarity measures for “word of mouth” recommendation. Ch. 3

  5. 1998
    GoTo.com: pay-per-click, pay-your-bid Post

    Search positions auctioned by bid, each winner paying its own bid per click. Bidders repeatedly outbid each other by a cent and then collapse to the minimum (bidding cycles), and ranking ignores click rates. Ch. 3

  6. 2000
    Google AdWords launches Post

    October 2000, self-service text ads, initially priced per thousand impressions, with about 350 advertisers. Ch. 3

  7. 2001
    Item-based collaborative filtering Paper

    Sarwar, Karypis, Konstan and Riedl, WWW: item–item neighbourhoods are more accurate and far cheaper online than user–user. Ch. 3

  8. 2002
    AdWords Select: rank by bid × CTR, pay the next price Post

    February 2002. Cost per click, ranking by predicted click-through rate (CTR) times bid, and an automatic discount that charges each winner just enough to keep its position: the generalized second-price (GSP) auction. Overture moves to GSP in mid-2002. Ch. 3

  9. 2002
    Learning to rank from clickthrough Paper

    Joachims, KDD: clicks as relative preference judgements, the start of the position-bias problem. Ch. 7

  10. 2003
    Amazon item-to-item filtering Paper

    Linden, Smith and York, IEEE Internet Computing: an offline similar-items table makes serving cost independent of catalogue size. Test-of-time award in 2017. Ch. 3

  11. 2003
    Contextual ads: AdSense Post

    Google matches ads to page content (March), launching self-serve AdSense in June. Yahoo buys Overture the same year for about $1.63 billion.

  12. 2005
    Quality Score sets minimum bids Post

    August 2005. Quality Score, Google's rating of an ad's expected click rate and relevance, sets the minimum bid each keyword needs to enter the auction. Ch. 3

  13. 2006
    Netflix Prize launches Post

    October 2006: $1M for a 10% improvement in root-mean-square error (RMSE) over Netflix's Cinematch on about 100 million ratings. Turns recommendation into a leaderboard. Ch. 4

  14. 2006
    Funk SVD Post

    Simon Funk's blog: stochastic gradient descent (SGD) on the observed ratings alone learns user and item factor vectors fast. Despite the name, not a singular value decomposition (SVD). Ch. 4

  15. 2007
    Predicting clicks for new ads Paper

    Richardson, Dominowska and Ragno, WWW (Microsoft): CTR for ads with no history, from text and term features. Ch. 4

  16. 2007
    GSP theory Paper

    Edelman, Ostrovsky and Schwarz (AER) and Varian (IJIO): the generalized second-price (GSP) auction is not truthful (bidding your value is not optimal), but in its lowest-revenue envy-free equilibrium (no advertiser wants to swap places and prices with the one above) it reproduces the Vickrey–Clarke–Groves (VCG) outcome. Ch. 3

  17. 2007
    Yahoo Panama and Google Conversion Optimizer Post

    Yahoo adds quality-weighted ranking in February; in September Google launches Conversion Optimizer, an early automatic bidder that aims at a maximum cost per acquisition (CPA) the advertiser sets. Ch. 6

  18. 2008
    Implicit-feedback alternating least squares (ALS) Paper

    Hu, Koren and Volinsky, ICDM: fit binary consumed/not-consumed labels over every user–item cell, weighted by a confidence that grows with the amount consumed, at a cost linear in the number of interactions because one item-factor product is shared by all users. Ch. 4

  19. 2008
    SVD++ Paper

    Koren, KDD: adds which items a user rated (implicit) to the factor model; timeSVD++ (2009) adds temporal drift. Ch. 4

  20. 2008
    Cascade click model Paper

    Craswell, Zoeter, Taylor and Ramsey, WSDM: users scan down and stop at the first satisfying result. Ch. 7

  21. 2008
    Netflix data de-anonymized Paper

    Narayanan and Shmatikov, IEEE S&P: “anonymous” ratings linked to public IMDb profiles. The 2010 sequel prize is cancelled. Ch. 4

  22. 2009
    Bayesian personalized ranking (BPR) Paper

    Rendle et al., UAI: pairwise ranking loss with sampled negatives, a smooth surrogate for AUC (area under the ROC curve). Ch. 4

  23. 2009
    Feature hashing Paper

    Weinberger et al., ICML: hash raw features into a fixed weight vector; no dictionary, tolerable collisions. Ch. 4

  24. 2009
    Netflix Prize awarded Post

    September 2009: BellKor's Pragmatic Chaos, test RMSE 0.8567 (10.06%). Netflix later adopts matrix factorization and restricted Boltzmann machines (RBMs), not the ensemble. Ch. 4

  25. 2010
    Factorization machines Paper

    Rendle, ICDM: a latent vector per feature, with every pairwise interaction computed in time linear in the number of features. Field-aware FMs (FFMs) later win Kaggle click-through-rate contests. Ch. 4

  26. 2010
    AdPredictor in Bing Paper

    Graepel et al., ICML: Bayesian probit regression with online updates, serving Bing sponsored search since 2009. Ch. 4

  27. 2010
    LinUCB on the Yahoo! front page Paper

    Li, Chu, Langford and Schapire, WWW: LinUCB (linear upper confidence bound), a contextual bandit that balances exploring and exploiting news articles, evaluated by replaying randomized logs (formalized in a 2011 WSDM paper). Ch. 4

  28. 2010
    YouTube's co-visitation recommender Paper

    Davidson et al., RecSys: item–item relatedness from co-visitation (videos watched in the same session), expanded through the resulting video graph, then ranked. Ch. 3

  29. 2010
    Real-time bidding standardizes Post

    The OpenRTB (open real-time bidding) project starts in late 2010; the Interactive Advertising Bureau (IAB) adopts OpenRTB 2.0 in January 2012. Exchanges price each impression in an auction. Ch. 6

  30. 2011
    Thompson sampling and doubly robust evaluation Paper

    Chapelle and Li (NIPS) show Thompson sampling works on ad data; Dudík, Langford and Li (ICML) introduce the doubly robust estimator for evaluating a new policy from an old policy's logs. Ch. 7

  31. 2012
    Optimized CPM at Facebook Post

    Optimized cost per mille (oCPM): advertisers bid for outcomes while the platform buys impressions with its own predictions; extended to conversions in November 2012. Ch. 6

  32. 2012
    YouTube optimizes for watch time Post

    March 2012 for related and recommended videos, October for search: the objective moves from clicks to time watched. Ch. 7

  33. 2012
    VCG for Google contextual ads Paper

    Late 2012, Google moves contextual (AdSense) ads to a version of the Vickrey–Clarke–Groves (VCG) auction, per Varian and Harris (2014); search stays on generalized second price (GSP). The same paper notes Facebook uses VCG. Ch. 3

  34. 2013
    FTRL-Proximal: “a view from the trenches” Paper

    McMahan et al., KDD: a follow-the-regularized-leader optimizer with per-coordinate learning rates and L1 sparsity, plus calibration monitoring and memory tricks, for Google's ad click-through-rate models. Ch. 4

  35. 2013
    Counterfactual reasoning at Bing Paper

    Bottou et al., JMLR: estimate the effect of ad-system changes from logs with randomized perturbations. Ch. 7

  36. 2013
    DSSM (Deep Structured Semantic Model): an early two-tower model for search Paper

    Huang et al., CIKM (Microsoft): query and document networks into a shared space, trained on clicks with a softmax over sampled negatives. Ch. 5

  37. 2014
    Facebook: gradient-boosted tree leaves into logistic regression Paper

    He et al., ADKDD: trees learn feature combinations offline, a linear model trains online; prediction quality drops measurably as the model ages. Ch. 4

  38. 2014
    Delayed conversion feedback Paper

    Chapelle, KDD: jointly model conversion probability and an exponentially distributed conversion delay, so recent clicks that have not converted yet are not treated as negatives. Ch. 7

  39. 2014
    Parameter server Paper

    Li et al., OSDI: sharded key–value parameter storage with asynchronous workers, the CPU-era training backbone for sparse models. Ch. 5

  40. 2015
    Header bidding and Prebid.js Post

    Publishers query several exchanges at once before calling their own ad server (the system that picks the ad); these chains of auctions undermine second-price rules. Ch. 6

  41. 2016
    Wide & Deep Paper

    Cheng et al. (Google Play): joint linear-crosses plus MLP. Online app acquisitions +3.9% over wide-only (deep-only +2.9%). Ch. 5

  42. 2016
    Deep Neural Networks for YouTube Paper

    Covington, Adams and Sargin, RecSys: softmax candidate generation served by nearest neighbours, watch-time-weighted ranking. Ch. 5

  43. 2016
    GRU4Rec Paper

    Hidasi et al., ICLR: recurrent networks (gated recurrent units, GRUs) for next-click prediction in sessions. Ch. 5

  44. 2016
    HNSW Paper

    Malkov and Yashunin: hierarchical navigable small-world graphs for fast approximate nearest-neighbour search. Ch. 5

  45. 2016
    Recommendations as treatments Paper

    Schnabel et al., ICML: reweight each observed rating by the inverse probability that it was observed (its propensity), because users choose what to rate. Ch. 7

  46. 2016
    Smart Bidding brand Post

    Google groups its automated conversion bidding (target cost per acquisition, CPA, since 2010; target return on ad spend, ROAS; and others) as Smart Bidding. Ch. 6

  47. 2017
    DeepFM and Deep & Cross Paper

    Huawei's DeepFM (IJCAI) shares embeddings between a factorization machine and a multilayer perceptron (MLP); Google's Deep & Cross Network (DCN, ADKDD) adds explicit cross layers. Ch. 5

  48. 2017
    Neural collaborative filtering Paper

    He et al., WWW: a multilayer perceptron replaces the matrix-factorization dot product; revisited and largely refuted in 2020. Ch. 5

  49. 2017
    FAISS (Facebook AI Similarity Search) Paper

    Johnson, Douze and Jégou: billion-scale similarity search on GPUs with quantization. Ch. 5

  50. 2017
    Unbiased learning to rank Paper

    Joachims, Swaminathan and Schnabel, WSDM: inverse-propensity-weighted clicks remove position bias. Ch. 7

  51. 2017
    oCPC at Taobao Paper

    Zhu et al., KDD: optimized cost per click (oCPC) adjusts the advertiser's cost-per-click bid within bounds by predicted conversion rate. Ch. 6

  52. 2017
    Safari Intelligent Tracking Prevention Post

    ITP, announced June and shipped with Safari 11 in September, restricts third-party (cross-site tracking) cookies using a classifier. Ch. 7

  53. 2018
    Deep Interest Network (DIN): candidate-aware attention Paper

    Zhou et al., KDD (Alibaba): weight past behaviours by relevance to the candidate ad, without softmax normalization. Ch. 5

  54. 2018
    ESMM and MMoE Paper

    Alibaba's Entire Space Multi-task Model (ESMM, SIGIR) trains conversion-rate prediction over all impressions as predicted click rate × predicted conversion rate; Google's Multi-gate Mixture-of-Experts (MMoE, KDD) shares expert subnetworks across tasks. Ch. 5

  55. 2018
    PinSage and SASRec Paper

    Pinterest's graph convolutional recommender on 3 billion nodes and 18 billion edges (KDD); self-attentive sequential recommendation (ICDM). Ch. 5

  56. 2018
    SKAdNetwork and GDPR Post

    Apple's SKAdNetwork, which reports app installs to ad networks only in aggregate and without a user identifier, arrives in iOS 11.3 (March); the EU's General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) is enforceable from May 25. Ch. 7

  57. 2018
    Meaningful social interactions Post

    January 2018: Facebook re-weights News Feed toward interactions between friends and family. Ch. 7

  58. 2019
    DLRM (Deep Learning Recommendation Model) Paper

    Naumov et al. (Meta): open reference model; embedding tables sharded across devices (model-parallel), dense layers replicated (data-parallel). Ch. 5

  59. 2019
    Google Ad Manager moves to first price Post

    Announced March, completed September: buyers must shade bids. Ch. 6

  60. 2019
    Autobidding with constraints Paper

    Aggarwal, Badanidiyuru and Mehta, WINE: bidding one multiple of each impression's value is optimal in truthful auctions (where bidding value is a dominant strategy); equilibrium welfare is at least half the optimum (price of anarchy 2). Ch. 6

  61. 2019
    Off-policy REINFORCE at YouTube Paper

    Chen et al., WSDM: policy-gradient training reweighted by the ratio of the new policy to the policy that produced the logs, with a correction for recommending several items at once. Ch. 7

  62. 2019
    Sampling-bias-corrected two towers Paper

    Yi et al., RecSys (YouTube): in-batch negatives with the logQ correction (subtract each item's log sampling probability) and item frequencies estimated on the fly from the training stream. Ch. 5

  63. 2019
    “Are we really making much progress?” Paper

    Dacrema, Cremonesi and Jannach, RecSys best paper: 7 of 18 neural methods reproducible, 6 of those often beaten by simple baselines. Ch. 5

  64. 2019
    Privacy Sandbox announced Post

    August 2019: Google proposes browser APIs to replace third-party cookies. Ch. 7

  65. 2020
    Embedding-based retrieval and ScaNN Paper

    Facebook's embedding-based retrieval (EBR) for search (KDD) and Google's ScaNN (Scalable Nearest Neighbors) anisotropic quantization (ICML). Ch. 5

  66. 2020
    SIM, LightGCN and PLE Paper

    Alibaba's Search-based Interest Model (SIM) on behaviour sequences up to 54,000 long; LightGCN, a simplified graph convolution network; Tencent's Progressive Layered Extraction (PLE) for multi-task learning. Ch. 5

  67. 2020
    Neural collaborative filtering (NCF) revisited; sampled metrics Paper

    Rendle et al. (RecSys): tuned dot products beat a learned multilayer-perceptron similarity. Krichene and Rendle (KDD): scoring against a small random sample of items instead of the whole catalogue can reverse which method looks best. Ch. 5

  68. 2021
    App Tracking Transparency Post

    iOS 14.5, April 26: cross-app tracking requires opt-in. User-level attribution on iOS largely ends. Ch. 7

  69. 2021
    DCN-V2 and Performance Max Paper

    DCN-V2, the Deep & Cross Network with matrix-valued cross layers, in production at Google (WWW); Performance Max, generally available in November, automates targeting, placement and bidding across Google surfaces. Ch. 6

  70. 2021
    Persia: 100 trillion parameters Paper

    Kuaishou and ETH Zürich: hybrid synchronous/asynchronous training; the parameters are almost all sparse embeddings. Ch. 8

  71. 2022
    Meta: ATT costs about $10B Call

    February 2, 2022 earnings call: the expected 2022 revenue headwind from iOS changes. Ch. 7

  72. 2022
    Monolith Paper

    ByteDance: collisionless cuckoo-hash embeddings and minute-level sync of sparse parameters for real-time training. Ch. 8

  73. 2022
    P5: recommendation as language Paper

    Geng et al., RecSys: several recommendation tasks as text-to-text prompts for one model. Ch. 8

  74. 2022
    Advantage+ shopping campaigns Post

    August 2022: Meta automates audience, placement and creative for e-commerce advertisers. Ch. 6

  75. 2023
    Twitter open-sources its recommender Post

    March 31: about 1,500 candidate tweets per request, scored by a ~48M-parameter main ranking model (the “heavy ranker”). Ch. 8

  76. 2023
    AppLovin AXON 2.0 Call

    The second version of AppLovin's ad-targeting and bidding engine, launched in the second quarter; the company attributes its subsequent ad-revenue growth to it. Ch. 6

  77. 2023
    TIGER: generative retrieval Paper

    Rajput et al., NeurIPS: TIGER (Transformer Index for GEnerative Recommenders) gives items semantic IDs, short code tuples from residual quantization of content embeddings, and a sequence-to-sequence model decodes the next item's ID. Ch. 8

  78. 2024
    HSTU: generative recommenders Paper

    Zhai et al., ICML (Meta): HSTU (hierarchical sequential transduction units) treats user actions as a sequence to predict; 1.5T parameters, +12.4% online, compute scaling law across three orders of magnitude. Ch. 8

  79. 2024
    Wukong scaling law Paper

    Zhang et al., ICML (Meta): stacked factorization-machine-style blocks scale across two orders of magnitude, past 100 GFLOP per example. Ch. 8

  80. 2024
    Chrome keeps third-party cookies Post

    July 22: Google drops cookie deprecation for user choice; in April 2025 it drops the choice prompt too. Ch. 7

  81. 2024
    Ads in AI Overviews Post

    October 3: ads appear in Google's AI-generated search answers on US mobile. Ch. 8

  82. 2024
    Meta Andromeda Post

    December 2: Meta's ads retrieval engine, with a claimed 10,000× increase in model capacity and, on selected segments, +6% recall (relevant ads retrieved) and +8% in Meta's ads-quality measure. Ch. 8

  83. 2025
    OneRec: end-to-end generative recommender Paper

    Kuaishou, February; one generative model replaces the retrieve-then-rank cascade; the June technical report claims about 25% of queries per second served, operating cost 10.6% of the cascade. Ch. 8

  84. 2025
    Netflix foundation model Post

    March: one large autoregressive model on member histories feeds downstream recommenders. Ch. 8

  85. 2025
    Meta GEM Call

    Generative Ads Model. July 30 call: about +5% ad conversions on Instagram and +3% on Facebook Feed and Reels in Q2; engineering post in November. Ch. 8

  86. 2025
    Privacy Sandbox retired Post

    October 17: Google retires most Privacy Sandbox APIs, including Topics (interest-based ads), Protected Audience (on-device remarketing auctions) and Attribution Reporting (noised conversion measurement). Ch. 7

  87. 2026
    Ads tested in ChatGPT Post

    January 16 announcement: tests for logged-in US adults on the Free and Go tiers; paid tiers stay ad-free. Ch. 8

No milestones match these filters.

Patterns worth noticing

  • Industry first, theory second, in auctions. GSP ran from 2002; its equilibrium analysis appeared in 2007. The same happened with autobidding: products from 2007, an equilibrium theory of autobidders from 2019.
  • Theory first, industry later, in evaluation. Propensity weighting and off-policy estimators existed for years before they appeared in production recommenders around 2017–2019.
  • Convergence around 2016. Before it, recommendation papers optimized RMSE and ranking metrics while ads papers optimized calibrated CTR; after it, the same architectures (Wide & Deep, DCN, DIN, MMoE) are used for both.
  • Exogenous shocks. The privacy entries are the only ones not driven by an algorithmic bottleneck, and they changed the ads track more than any single model did.
